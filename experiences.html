01 — The dome

An hour at the eyepiece, or four.

Roux's 1931 Zeiss is still on its original pier, now driven by a motor rather than a hand crank. You book it the way you would book a table — by the hour, from nine until three, with Dr Vidal beside you or with nobody at all.

In winter the deep sky is absurd: the Tarantula, Omega Centauri, 47 Tucanae, all of them high and steady. In summer the planets come up over the ridge around midnight. There is a heated room ten steps away with a thermos in it, and nobody minds how long you take.

02 — Table Nine

Twelve seats, one sitting, no menu.

Mateo Alfaro writes the evening at about eleven each morning, once the altiplano growers have come up the road and he has seen what they brought. Quinoa from 4,000 metres, rica-rica, chañar, copao fruit, and lamb from a valley two hours south. It is not a tasting menu so much as a report on the week.

Dinner is at 20:30, before the sky is properly dark, which is deliberate. The pass faces the window. In August you can watch the plateau go out while the third course lands.

03 — The rock baths

Thirty-eight, thirty-four, twelve.

Three pools cut into the hillside in 1972, fed by the spring at the temperature it arrives. Nothing here is heated and nothing is chilled — the difference between the pools is depth and shade, which is the oldest trick there is.

There is a hammam below them, a cold room, and four treatment cabins where the work is done with rica-rica oil pressed on the property. The baths are unlit after eleven, which is when they are best.

04 — Out there

Six ways to spend a day off the ridge.

All of them included, all of them optional, none of them run to a schedule you did not agree to the night before.

01

The salt flats

Out at 05:20 in a canvas-topped Land Cruiser. Flamingos on the brine by seven, breakfast on a folding table by eight, back before the heat.

02

The long walk

Eleven kilometres on the old mule track to the abandoned nitrate station, with a guide who knows where the water is and what the graffiti says.

03

The geysers

A four o'clock start for the field at 4,300 metres, where the fumaroles only show in the cold. Thermos, blankets, and altitude tablets in the door pocket.

04

Silent hours

Not an activity. From 23:00 the house drops to amber and stops talking. It is the thing guests write to us about most, months later.

05

The library

Hélène's 2,300 volumes, catalogued and readable, in the Meissa Apartment. Ask at the desk when it is unoccupied; you will usually be let in.

06

Nothing at all

A chair on the west terrace faces the plateau and points at no view in particular. It is the most heavily used amenity in the house.

What it costs
